  {\Large \textbf{MED 610 Examination}}\\[0.25em]
  {\large \textbf{Clinical Reasoning and Differential Diagnosis -- Answer Key}}\\[0.5em]
\end{center}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]

  \item \textbf{(B)} Aortic dissection.\\
  Classic presentation: sudden, severe, tearing chest pain radiating to the back, with blood pressure 
  differential between arms (indicating involvement of subclavian artery). This is a medical emergency 
  requiring immediate imaging (CT angiography or transesophageal echo) and management.

  \item \textbf{(B)} Diabetic ketoacidosis.\\
  Young patient with hyperglycemia, polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, and ketonuria suggests 
  type 1 diabetes presenting with DKA. The presence of ketones distinguishes this from simple 
  hyperglycemia or hyperosmolar state (which typically occurs in type 2 diabetes).

  \item \textbf{(C)} Obstructive jaundice (posthepatic).\\
  Elevated conjugated (direct) bilirubin with elevated alkaline phosphatase indicates biliary 
  obstruction. Dark urine (conjugated bilirubin excretion) and pale stools (absent stercobilin) 
  confirm obstruction. Causes include gallstones, pancreatic cancer, or biliary strictures.

  \item \textbf{(B)} Squamous cell carcinoma.\\
  Central location with hilar involvement is characteristic of squamous cell carcinoma, which arises 
  from central bronchi. It's strongly associated with smoking. Small cell is also central but more 
  aggressive with early metastasis. Adenocarcinoma is typically peripheral.

  \item \emph{Sample answer:}\\
  \textbf{Systematic approach to acute abdominal pain}:

  \textbf{1. Anatomical consideration} (by quadrant):
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RUQ}: Cholecystitis, hepatitis, liver abscess
    \item \textbf{RLQ}: Appendicitis, ectopic pregnancy, ovarian torsion, Crohn's disease
    \item \textbf{LUQ}: Splenic rupture, gastric ulcer, pancreatitis
    \item \textbf{LLQ}: Diverticulitis, ectopic pregnancy, ovarian torsion
    \item \textbf{Epigastric}: Peptic ulcer, pancreatitis, MI
    \item \textbf{Diffuse}: Peritonitis, mesenteric ischemia, bowel obstruction
  \end{itemize}

  \textbf{2. Character of pain}:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Colicky (intermittent): Obstruction (kidney stone, bowel obstruction)
    \item Constant, severe: Perforation, ischemia, severe inflammation
    \item Burning: Peptic ulcer disease, GERD
  \end{itemize}

  \textbf{3. Associated symptoms}:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Fever: Infection (appendicitis, diverticulitis, cholecystitis)
    \item Vomiting: Obstruction, acute abdomen
    \item Diarrhea: Gastroenteritis, inflammatory bowel disease
    \item Hematemesis/melena: GI bleeding
  \end{itemize}

  \textbf{4. Initial tests}:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Labs: CBC, CMP, lipase, liver enzymes, pregnancy test (females), urinalysis
    \item Imaging: Upright chest X-ray (free air), abdominal X-ray, ultrasound, CT scan
  \end{itemize}

  \item \emph{Sample answer:}\\
  \textbf{Differential diagnosis of heart failure}:

  \textbf{Left-sided heart failure}:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Causes}: Ischemic heart disease, hypertension, aortic/mitral valve disease, cardiomyopathy
    \item \textbf{Features}: Dyspnea, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea (PND), pulmonary edema 
    (rales on auscultation), S3 gallop
    \item \textbf{Mechanism}: Inability to pump blood forward $\to$ pulmonary congestion
  \end{itemize}

  \textbf{Right-sided heart failure}:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Causes}: Left heart failure (most common), pulmonary hypertension, tricuspid/pulmonary 
    valve disease, cor pulmonale
    \item \textbf{Features}: Jugular venous distension (JVD), hepatomegaly, ascites, peripheral edema, 
    hepatojugular reflux
    \item \textbf{Mechanism}: Inability to pump blood forward from right ventricle $\to$ systemic venous 
    congestion
  \end{itemize}

  \textbf{Biventricular failure}:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Combination of left and right heart failure features
    \item Most common presentation in advanced heart failure
  \end{itemize}

  \textbf{This patient}: Has features of both left (dyspnea, orthopnea) and right (JVD, edema) heart 
  failure, indicating biventricular failure.

  \item \emph{Sample answer:}\\
  \textbf{Fever of Unknown Origin (FUO)}: Fever $>38.3^\circ$C for more than 3 weeks without diagnosis 
  despite initial evaluation.

  \textbf{Major categories}:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Infections} (30--40\%): Tuberculosis, endocarditis, abscesses, HIV/opportunistic infections
    \item \textbf{Malignancies} (20--30\%): Lymphoma, leukemia, renal cell carcinoma
    \item \textbf{Autoimmune/inflammatory} (10--20\%): Still's disease, SLE, vasculitis, inflammatory 
    bowel disease
    \item \textbf{Miscellaneous}: Drug fever, factitious fever, pulmonary embolism
  \end{enumerate}

  \textbf{Initial workup}:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{History}: Travel, exposures, medications, risk factors
    \item \textbf{Exam}: Careful physical including lymph nodes, skin, heart murmurs, hepatosplenomegaly
    \item \textbf{Labs}: CBC with differential, ESR/CRP, blood cultures (multiple sets), urinalysis, 
    LFTs, ANA, RF
    \item \textbf{Imaging}: Chest X-ray, CT chest/abdomen/pelvis (look for lymphadenopathy, masses, abscesses)
    \item \textbf{Special tests}: PPD/QuantiFERON (TB), HIV test, echocardiogram (endocarditis), 
    lumbar puncture if indicated
  \end{itemize}

  Consider empiric antibiotics only if patient is hemodynamically unstable.

  \item \emph{Sample answer:}\\
  \textbf{Differential diagnosis of altered mental status in elderly}:

  \textbf{Key distinction - Delirium vs. Dementia}:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Delirium}: Acute onset (hours to days), fluctuating course, inattention prominent, 
    reversible with treatment of underlying cause
    \item \textbf{Dementia}: Chronic onset (months to years), progressive, insidious, memory impairment 
    prominent, usually irreversible
  \end{itemize}

  \textbf{Acute medical causes to urgently rule out}:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Metabolic}: Hypoglycemia, hyponatremia, hypernatremia, hypercalcemia, hepatic 
    encephalopathy, uremia
    \item \textbf{Infectious}: UTI, pneumonia, sepsis, meningitis/encephalitis
    \item \textbf{Cardiovascular}: Stroke, MI, heart failure, arrhythmias causing hypoperfusion
    \item \textbf{Respiratory}: Hypoxia from any cause
    \item \textbf{Medications}: Anticholinergics, benzodiazepines, opioids, polypharmacy
    \item \textbf{Withdrawal}: Alcohol, benzodiazepines
    \item \textbf{Neurological}: Seizures (postictal state), subdural hematoma, increased ICP
    \item \textbf{Endocrine}: Thyroid dysfunction (myxedema coma, thyroid storm), Addisonian crisis
  \end{itemize}

  \textbf{Approach}:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item ABCDE assessment, vital signs
    \item Finger-stick glucose immediately
    \item Labs: CBC, CMP, calcium, TSH, urinalysis, blood cultures
    \item CT head (rule out stroke, hemorrhage, mass)
    \item Consider lumbar puncture if meningitis suspected
  \end{itemize}

  Remember: Delirium often superimposed on dementia in elderly. Treat reversible causes aggressively.

\end{enumerate}
